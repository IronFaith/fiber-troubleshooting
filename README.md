# Optic / fiber health

Live site: https://ironfaith.github.io/fiber-troubleshooting/

A small, dependency-free Yes/No troubleshooting diagram. Start with a direct optic loop or a loop at a passive. Each screen shows one action, a short test-path sketch where useful, and one result question. Branches preview the next action. The compact trail retains the chosen answers and connects them to the current action. Click a previous step or Back to revise a result; later answers are discarded. Restart clears the path; changing loop location starts a fresh path. Session storage preserves the selected mode and valid history.

A passive loop covers the optic plus the tested fiber and connections to its loop point. A passing local loop leads to the next accessible passive; the remote optic is never a prerequisite. When no further passive is reachable, restore the path and check local health. If the link still fails, trace only segments that can be isolated and accessed, then measure loss where possible. Inaccessible sections and the remote optic remain unverified, with a result that records the limit of local testing. A failed passive loop leads to direct optic isolation, not an immediate optic-failure conclusion. A valid direct-loop failure leads to inspection/cleaning and a controlled known-good comparison. A VFL pass does not prove an acceptable optical loss budget. Test availability is not assumed: answer only after a valid site-approved test.

The site's workflow uses an LC loopback directly at the optic and an MMC loopback at the passive. Blue outgoing and green returning paths show TX returning to RX. A moving arrow follows the light path, with a pause/play control; reduced-motion preferences start the animation paused, with Play available. The animation illustrates the test, not live telemetry. The MMC leader / LC follower illustration shows one test pair within a group, not a pin map, fiber count or connector specification. Use the site's exact lane mapping and supported loopback/attenuation. CAS 1 Port 12 is the user's generic example, not an actual connection record. Shared hardware drawings show the site's 32-port cassette with 1–16 across the top and 17–32 across the bottom, an inserted loopback at port 12, and an enlarged side view of its connection. Direct LC loops show two plugged-in connectors and a physical return cord on the upright optic. These are educational illustrations, not dimensional models or connector pin maps. The same loop drawings appear in troubleshooting, chapter 1 and the quizzes; chapter 3 uses the numbered cassette in its animated LC-to-MMC path and its Match / Prepare / Seat sequence. The enlarged MMC head slides into the adapter on Seat, with a visible latch confirmation; selecting Seat again replays it. Motion controls and reduced-motion preferences apply.

Color-coded RX/TX badges illustrate the user's reported no-light reading (−40) and a good reading (+0.25 dBm). They are examples, not live readings or universal pass/fail thresholds; each lane must meet the applicable site and optic limits. The app contains no uploaded photos or real device IDs and does not connect to Cable Guy. It has no link to the separate EOD app.

The fiber steps retain a schematic of the current isolated segment: disconnected optic, VFL source, expected port and alternate port. Answers distinguish untested, light seen, no light at the expected port, and light at another port. A missing-light result highlights the whole span and its connections without inventing a break location. Confirmed traces advance the segment count; the next section starts unverified. Back, answer edits and reloads reconstruct the picture from history. Segment numbers follow the user's trace sequence, not actual CAS identifiers; use the site layout for real port mapping. VFL shows continuity, not acceptable attenuation.

Technical references: [Cisco optical troubleshooting](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/switches/catalyst-9200-switch-stack/222738-troubleshoot-fiber-links-on-catalyst-900.html), [Fluke fiber troubleshooting](https://www.flukenetworks.com/edocs/white-paper-troubleshooting-fiber), [US Conec MMC polarity](https://www.usconec.com/featured-products/mmc-connector-solutions/speed-of-deployment).

## Development

Animated blue paths represent invisible optic IR; red paths represent VFL test light. A single play/pause control respects reduced-motion preferences. Interactive figures compare loop locations, clean/dirty/unseated connections, excessive bending and VFL outcomes. Returning to troubleshooting restores the existing session path. These are educational illustrations, not live measurements, literal jacket glow or site pin maps.

Requires Node.js; no dependencies or build step.

The beginner knowledge check (`quiz.html`) presents five questions per round: Spot the problem, Check the connection, Understand the light, then Narrow down the problem. It starts with the user's red −40 no-light indication and possible dirt/seating/bend causes; terminology and numeric limits arrive in round 3. The 20 questions use short scenarios with plausible beginner misconceptions, multiple possible causes and five picture-based selections: connection seating, CAS 2 Port 28, the local RX direction, a dark lane and the added section after a failed passive loop. Learners see observations before answering, then explanatory highlights, a short reason and a lesson link. Text questions have two or three choices; the possible-causes question accepts multiple choices. Each round stops after five answers. A completed round with at least four correct offers the next five without advancing automatically; missed questions can be retried separately, or the same five repeated. Successful retries count toward round progress while preserving the original first-try score in the results. A stop link returns to lessons. The last round has no further-round button. All four quizzes can be chosen in any order. Best completed results and Fiber Rookie stripes persist in the same browser/device. Each different quiz passed earns one stripe; repeats cannot add duplicate stripes or lower a best result. Unfinished attempts restart when left. The results include a skills recap based on answers from this round; a missed check keeps its skill in Practice next until corrected in a retry. Existing best scores and Fiber Rookie stripes are preserved. The check is educational, not a field qualification.

Numeric quiz limits (TX −1 to +3 dBm and RX −3 to +3 dBm) are explicitly fictional training limits, not installed-optic specifications or a universal site threshold. The quiz includes the user's −40 no-light indication and +0.25 example, teaches lower and upper bounds, checks each lane, and distinguishes a power reading from proof of complete link health. VFL questions distinguish continuity/mapping from measured loss. The shared Learn menu links to the check from every page.
